Notice of Violation CV‑2026‑126219

Nine Violations

Three weeks after Austin closed its case on 7609 Creekbluff Dr as voluntary compliance, a council office pressed, and the City went back. The notice it issued on September 24 cites nine violations — and for the first time in nine years, it cites the inside of the house.

By Mark Garcia, President, Bull Creek Home Owners Association
From the reopened case file and correspondence with the District 10 council office
Published November 23, 2026

On August 31, 2026, Austin Development Services closed its code case on 7609 Creekbluff Dr, recorded all three cited violations as cleared, and entered the property as compliant. Four days later I published the photographs showing two of those three conditions exactly as cited, and sent the record to Council Member Duchen's office. Three weeks of correspondence followed. On September 21 an inspector returned to the property; on September 24 the City issued a new Notice of Violation under a new case number, written by an inspector who had not worked the property before. It is not a reissue of what was closed. It is a different document in kind.

Where the June notice cited three exterior-maintenance items, CV‑2026‑126219 cites nine violations across three violation types — including rodent infestation inside the structure, interior drywall, window and door frames, walking surfaces, and residential construction performed without a permit. Every structural remedy on it carries the same condition: building permits will be needed prior to repairs starting.

I. Three weeks of correspondence

The sequence matters, because the City's own account of the property changed twice inside three weeks.

On September 4 I wrote to Council Member Duchen, Communications Director Anne-Charlotte Patterson and Chief of Staff Carrie Smith, setting out what the public-information file showed: that the August closure rested on a one-sentence note, that the elevation cited under §304.6 was never photographed on the day it was cleared, and that my own photographs taken that morning showed the conditions in place.1 I asked the office to press Development Services to reopen the case, to state the basis on which §304.6 was cleared, and to re-serve any live notice at the registered agent's address on file with the Secretary of State.

No one replied. On September 19 I wrote again: "I have not received a response from anyone."

On September 21 the office responded. The reply listed three citations — unsanitary conditions, exterior walls, protective treatment — and said a Notice of Violation had been sent, that the office would "continue to observe the condition of the violations," and that "if we do not have voluntary compliance within the allotted compliance timeframe, the case will be transitioned to legal escalation."2

The reply described a case that had already been closed

The three citations listed were the three items of CV‑2026‑067670 — the notice issued June 2 and closed as voluntary compliance on August 31, three weeks before the reply was written. The compliance timeframes described as pending ("7 days to 30 days for the structural conditions") had expired on June 9 and July 2. The council office was relaying as current a case the department had already marked complete, which was the precise failure the September 4 email had documented.

The same reply also stated that "as these issues have not been resolved we are working with code enforcement and city legal to escalate the non-compliance of the property owner," and added: "I apologize that these escalations did not occur in a more-timely manner."

On September 22 I replied that the office appeared to be describing a citation the code department had already closed as completed while the conditions remained, and that the neighborhood's frustration was with the absence of coordinated follow-up across nine years.

On September 24, William Grant, Senior Policy Strategist for Council Member Duchen, wrote back:

William Grant, Senior Policy Strategist, District 10 · September 24, 2026Dear Mark Garcia, I am sorry that you have found this process so frustrating and that there isn't enough follow-though. I will take responsibility to walk this issue through process. New citations have been issued yesterday and when they are not resolved City Code will escalate to judicial action. I will keep you up to date on timelines as I have them.

That is the clearest commitment any City office has made on this property in nine years of correspondence: a named staff member taking ownership of the process, and an explicit statement that unresolved citations will go to judicial action rather than be closed.

III. Why this notice is different

1. It cites the inside of the house

For nine years every code section the City cited at this property was an exterior-maintenance item or a permit item. That was the central finding of the analysis published in September: across 43 logged inspections, not one assessed the interior, and the department's own closing note of December 11, 2025 recorded that "the work inside structure was never verified."4

CV‑2026‑126219 cites §305.3 Interior surfaces, requiring interior drywall to be repaired or replaced, and §309.1 Infestation, requiring elimination of a rodent infestation "located inside the Single-Family house." Both are findings that can only be made from inside the structure. After nine years, an inspector appears to have gone in.

2. Rodents, and the damage they have done

The infestation citation carries the shortest deadline on the notice — seven days — and it is corroborated elsewhere in the same document: the §304.7 roofing item requires the owner to "repair rodent damaged facia and soffit." The condition is recorded as affecting both the interior and the building envelope. Nothing in the 266-page public-information file released on September 2 mentions rodents anywhere.

3. Repairs now require permits first

Every structural remedy on the notice ends with the same two sentences: "Building permits will be needed prior to repairs starting. Re-Activate BP: 2019‑237817 by contacting Development Services Department-Permitting."

Why that clause matters

The August closure turned on an inspector's unsupported sentence — "The repairs made to the exterior of the structure" — with no permit, no inspection and no description of the work. A violation whose remedy requires a permit cannot close that way. The notice states it directly: "An inspection on any required permits will need to be conducted before the above code violation(s) can be closed."

It also revives the permit question itself. §25‑12‑241 cites "residential construction performed without required permit(s)" and requires reactivation of building permit 2019‑237817 — the same expired "express" permit, authorising only removal of sheetrock and insulation for exploratory purposes, that has been at the centre of this property's file since 2019.

4. The porch is now a structural item

In June the porch posts were cited under §304.2 Protective Treatment — a surfaces-and-coatings provision whose remedy was to "replace/repair front porch post trims." They are now cited under §304.10, which requires decks, porches and balconies to be "maintained structurally sound, in good repair, with proper anchorage and capable of supporting the imposed loads," with the remedy restated as replacing or repairing the porch columns. That is a reclassification from finish to structure.

IV. The service problem was not fixed

One request in the September 4 email has not been met. Both certified copies of the June notice came back undelivered — the owner's copy endorsed "ATTEMPTED NOT KNOWN, UNABLE TO FORWARD" — and I asked the City to re-serve any live notice on the registered agent at the address on file with the Texas Secretary of State, 3100 Kramer Ln Apt 534, rather than only the appraisal-district mailing address.

The copy of CV‑2026‑126219 obtained from the City is addressed to 7609 Creekbluff LLC, PO Box 684196, Austin TX 78768‑4196 — the same post-office box the June notice was returned from, sent by certified mail under article 9589 0710 5270 3994 0651 20.

Stated carefully

The document obtained is the owner copy. The City may also have issued a registered-agent copy that is not part of what was downloaded, and the record here does not show whether this notice was delivered or returned. What it does show is that the owner copy went to the same address that failed in June. Whether service succeeded this time is a question for the department, and it bears directly on the transfer restriction and the twenty-day appeal window the notice carries.

V. Where the deadlines stand

Every compliance window on the notice has now expired.

  • Oct 1, 2026Seven-day deadline expires — rodent infestation (§309.1) and trash removal (§10‑5‑21).
  • Oct 8, 2026Fourteen-day deadline expires — reactivate building permit 2019‑237817 (§25‑12‑241).
  • Oct 14, 2026Twenty-day window to appeal to the Building and Standards Commission expires.
  • Oct 24, 2026Thirty-day deadline expires — the five remaining structural items.
  • Nov 23, 2026Case CV‑2026‑126219 remains in an active state. Thirty days have passed since the final deadline.

The notice sets out what is available on expiry: criminal charges in Municipal Court of up to $2,000 per violation per day; civil penalties in an administrative hearing of up to $1,000 per violation per day; suspension or cancellation of permits, with utility disconnection; civil injunctions in State court; and, for dangerous or substandard buildings, action through the Building and Standards Commission to order the vacation, securing, repair, removal or demolition of a building.

Mr. Grant's commitment was that "when they are not resolved City Code will escalate to judicial action." Nine violations are now unresolved past their deadlines. The case staying open past the deadline is itself a departure from this property's history — every previous case closed rather than escalated.

VI. What to watch

Open questions

1. Was CV‑2026‑126219 served successfully, and was a copy sent to the registered agent at the Secretary of State address?

2. Has building permit 2019‑237817 been reactivated, or a new permit applied for, since September 24?

3. Has the rodent infestation cited under §309.1 been abated, and on what evidence — the seven-day deadline expired on October 1.

4. Given that the City has now cited interior conditions and an infestation, has the structure been assessed against the unsafe- and substandard-building provisions, which carry the Building and Standards Commission route?

5. When will the judicial escalation Mr. Grant described be initiated, and under which mechanism — Municipal Court, administrative hearing, or the BSC?

Method & limits

The account of the new notice is taken from the City's own document, obtained from Austin Development Services and published in full above. Code sections, remedies, dates, deadlines and the certified-mail article number are transcribed from it. Compliance deadlines are computed from the date of notice as the notice directs.

The correspondence summarised in section I is a single email thread between the author and the District 10 council office running from May to September 2026. City staff are quoted verbatim from that thread and identified by name and title, as public employees acting in their official capacity. The author's own messages are summarised rather than reproduced, and the thread is not published as a raw file, to keep private contact details out of the public record.

Statements about what changed between the June and September notices are comparisons of two documents. Where the record does not establish a fact — whether the September notice was delivered, whether a registered-agent copy exists, what prompted the interior findings — this report says so rather than inferring. It does not allege criminal conduct by any person or entity, and makes no claim about the intent of any City employee or property owner.
